EG-4.5 Extender Board

Low-frequency extension preamp for an EG-4.5-II geophone: 4.5 Hz element flattened down to 0.31 Hz, band-limited at 40 Hz, into an ADS1220 on a Raspberry Pi. Two boards on ElectroCookie solderable breadboards. Drawings, tables and test steps; the short text version, with the ordering list and the cold and powered checks, is README.md beside this file.

Parts

Main board first, then the interface board. Confirm AD797ANZ and LT1113CN8 are in stock in DIP-8 before ordering anything else. Film capacitors: J (±5%) for C1, C1b, Cf1 and Cf2; K is fine for Chp. In the TDK range the series number is the pitch — B32529 = 5 mm, B32520 = 7.5 mm.

Main board

28 parts, 15 jumpers plus the board’s own power jumper. Hole names are the board’s printing: row letter then column, so E9 is row E, column 9. The rails are used as V+ / GND / GND / V− top to bottom; ignore the printed + and −, and leave the edge solder-jumper pads open on both boards — they would short a rail to ground.

Orange lines are jumpers; the dashed one at column 1 is the board’s power jumper tying the two ground rails. Pale lines are component leads. Orange numbers on the chips are DIP pin numbers; note the pin-1 dot. White arrows on J1, J2 and J3 mark the terminals’ wire openings. Cc is drawn on the R3 strips (I8/H15) but is soldered directly onto R3’s leads.

Main board — assembly and test

Four stages, each ending in a check. The checks matter more than the order.

STAGE 1 Before the iron is hot

  1. stopMeter the rails. Top-outer to bottom-inner and top-inner to bottom-outer both read open; each of the four strips is continuous from column 1 to 30. The pad pairs at mid-height on each short edge are open solder-jumpers — leave them open.
  2. Measure every resistor and bag them by designator. R4 (301 Ω) and Rf1/Rf2 (17.8 kΩ) are easy to mix up.
  3. Check the five film capacitors are film, not ceramic. Chp and C1 are the same 2.2 µF part; C1b is 1 µF; Cf1 220 nF and Cf2 100 nF.
  4. Do not insert the chips yet. They go in at stage 4, after the rails are proven.

STAGE 2 Wiring and passives

Low profile to high.

  1. Solder the jumpers: the power jumper at column 1 between the two ground rails, then jumpers 1 to 15 from the table. Any hole on the same strip is the same node.
  2. Fit the two 8-pin sockets: U1 across columns 6–9, U2 across 18–21, straddling the channel with pin 1 at the lower left.
  3. Fit the parts that stand on end: R4 c7, Rsh c8, R4b c19, Rhp c20 (row A down to the bottom ground rail); Cf2 J24 up to the top ground rail; Rd1 J1 up to V+ and Rd2 G5 down to V−.
  4. Fit the remaining resistors: R3 J15–J8, Rs F15–F12, R3b A23–A26, Rf2 H24–H26, Rf1 D26–F26 (crosses the channel). Then solder Cc directly across R3’s leads.
  5. Fit the four film capacitors on edge: C1 H12–H8, Chp C16–C20, C1b C23–C26, Cf1 J26–J28.
  6. Fit the rail capacitors: Cd1 c4 and Cd3 c15 across the top pair, Cd2 c11 and Cd4 c23 across the bottom pair. Then the electrolytics at column 6: Cb1 V+ to GND with + on V+, Cb2 GND to V− with + on GND. Bend their leads in 0.5 mm each.
  7. Fit the three screw terminals last: J1 C1/C3, J2 I1/I3/I5, J3 G28/G30, wire openings toward the nearer board edge.
  8. checkCold meter check — no power, sockets empty, nothing on J1 or J3.

STAGE 3 Power, with the sockets empty

Build and test the interface board first (next section); it supplies the ±5 V.

  1. Wire J_PWR to J2, power off: J_PWR column 26 → J2 column 1 (+5 V), 28 → 3 (ground), 30 → 5 (−5 V). Leave J1 and J3 disconnected.
  2. Feed 5 V into the interface board’s J_IN from any 5 V source. Check polarity first; PS1 has no reverse protection.
  3. checkMeasure at the empty sockets, black lead on a ground rail: U1 pin 7 and U2 pin 8 positive, U1 pin 4 and U2 pin 4 negative. Unloaded they read high (the built board: +5.78 / −6.12 V); that is normal.
  4. Power down. Insert the AD797 in U1 and the LT1113 in U2, pin 1 to the lower left.
  5. checkPower up and re-read the rails. With both chips drawing, the rails pull down and equalise (±5.47 V measured before the Rd/Cb filter was added; expect about 0.2 V less now). If they stay where they were unloaded, the chips are not drawing: pin 1 is in the wrong corner.

STAGE 4 Bring-up

  1. Power up with J1 still disconnected and wait a full minute. The DC block has a 3.3 s time constant ahead of a ×534 boost; the output takes 30 s or more to settle.
  2. checkMeasure DC at J3-1. Inside ±50 mV, wandering; about 18.7 mA per rail. If the output sits near a rail, suspect Chp (an electrolytic there leaks enough to saturate stage B).
  3. Connect the geophone to J1 — twisted pair; ground a shield at the board end only.
  4. checkTap the floor near the geophone. Several hundred mV to a volt at J3-1, ringing for a second or two. No response means the geophone, J1 or the GEO net.
  5. Connect J3 to J_SIG on the interface board: J3 column 28 (OUT) → J_SIG column 27, J3 column 30 (AGND) → J_SIG column 29. Then fit the ADS1220 module and go to Raspberry Pi.

Interface board

Takes 5 V from the Pi header, makes the isolated ±5 V for the main board, holds the 2.5 V reference and the level shifter, and carries the ADS1220 module. Rails here are +5 V, AGND, AGND, −5 V top to bottom. J_IN’s two pins are the converter’s isolated input and go nowhere else. The instrument’s ground meets the Pi’s at exactly one place: the module’s DGND pin, through J_PI.

Assembly and test

  1. stopMeter the rails first, as on the main board: top-outer to bottom-inner and top-inner to bottom-outer both open. Leave the edge solder-jumper pads open.
  2. Trial-fit the module with header pins in it, then take it out again — it goes in last.
  3. Solder the jumpers: the power jumper at column 1, then 1 to 16 from the table, any order. Jumper 12 crosses the channel from F23 to E24.
  4. Fit the two 8-pin female header strips at D13–D20 and I13–I20, and the 8-pin male header J_PI at J13–J20. Tack one pin, check square, then the rest.
  5. Fit VR1 at I22/I23/I24. Pins 1 and 3 are both AGND so it cannot go in backwards, but the middle pin (cathode) must land on I23.
  6. Fit the five resistors: Rfd G23–G21, Rb C22–C27, Ra A22–A24, Rap D21–D24, Rbp A21 standing up to the bottom rail.
  7. Fit the three capacitors on end: Cdif E22–E21, Ccm1 A20 to the rail, Ccm2 A19 to the rail.
  8. Fit PS1 across columns 6–11 in row F, pin 1 (+Vin) at column 6. Check the marking dot; a reversed input destroys it.
  9. Fit the three terminals last: J_IN I1/I3, J_PWR I26/I28/I30 (facing the top edge), J_SIG A27/A29 (facing the bottom edge).

Software

  1. Install. Raspberry Pi OS Lite, SPI enabled in raspi-config, sudo apt install python3-numpy python3-scipy python3-spidev plus python3-rpi-lgpio (Pi 5; works everywhere) or python3-rpi.gpio. That runs the driver and the bench test. The logging daemon, its venv with obspy, the systemd unit and the PC client are in ../software/README.md.
  2. Run ads1220_capture.py. It writes the registers, reads them back (raising on mismatch), times DRDY for three seconds and builds the 60 Hz notch, 0.05 Hz DC block and decimator from the measured rate. The built board runs at 323.6 SPS, 1.9% under nominal — the ADS1220’s own oscillator. Thousands of SPS means DRDY is not wired (J_PI column 20 to Pi pin 13) and the driver aborts.
  3. Counts to velocity: 24-bit two’s complement, sign-extend bit 23, then velocity_m_s = count × 1.4103e-10 — (2.048/4)/223 V per count over 433 V/(m/s). Logging runs at 165 SPS via DECIMATE = 2 (6th-order Butterworth at 55 Hz ahead of it).
  4. Let it settle. The first minute after power-up is not data.
  5. stopDo not run geophone_response.Compensator on this data. The board already does the low-frequency extension in hardware; applying both turns the 0.31 Hz corner into a drift amplifier.

Notes

The short list: what will bite if you change it.

1

Chp and C1b must be film

Stage B has a DC gain of 23 behind Rhp’s 1.5 MΩ; an electrolytic’s leakage there parks the output near a rail.

2

Keep A-INV tight and the input short

R3, Rs and R4 cluster at U1 pin 2; J1 → Rsh → U1-3 stays under 15 mm and away from the output side.

3

Star the ground at J2

R4, Rhp, R4b, Cf2 and the J1 return go to the ground rail near J2, not daisy-chained.

4

The 60 Hz filter is not a hum filter

It is an anti-alias stage for the sensor’s 140 Hz spurious. Mains is notched in software; use twisted pair and ground a shield at one end only.

5

Slow start-up is normal

30–60 s to settle after power-up.

6

Bias from the reference, not a rail

The Pi’s 5 V moves by tens of mV in the band; the LM4040 does not. Only the 0.1% match of Ra/Ra′ and Rb/Rb′ rejects what is left.

7

Cc, Rd1/Rd2, Cb1/Cb2 were added 2026-09-16

After the sub-1 Hz floor measured well above the design budget. Cc stabilises U1; the 10 Ω + 220 µF filters take the converter’s switching ripple 40 dB down. The 0.5 Hz floor halved.

8

Calibration

The 433 V/(m/s) figure rests on the element’s datasheet sensitivity and on Rsh. If you can borrow any calibrated seismometer, put it on the same floor, record both for an hour and compare in 10–30 Hz; the amplitude ratio is your correction. Without one, measure the element (README.md, Parts) and trust the arithmetic.
